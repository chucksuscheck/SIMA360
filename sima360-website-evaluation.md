# SIMA360.org — Website Evaluation
## Framework Platform & Operational Ecosystem Review

---

## 1. Executive Assessment

### Overall Strategic Alignment: Poor

The website currently functions as a framework marketing site. It explains what the components are. It does not operationalize anything. A user can read every page and leave with a clear product map but no deeper understanding of the framework's intellectual architecture — which is precisely the relationship the site must establish if it is to function as the operational extension of the book.

### Overall Ecosystem Coherence: Partial

The five components (Core, Probe, Flow, Kit, Ascend) are all present and each page describes its own role with reasonable clarity. What is almost entirely missing is the inter-component logic: why these five things in this order, what happens at the handoff points, and how they constitute an integrated system rather than a product catalog.

### Relationship Between Book and Website: Absent

The book is not mentioned anywhere on the site. There is no page, section, or even sentence that positions the book as the intellectual foundation for what the site operationalizes. This is the single most strategically damaging gap. Without it, the site reads as a standalone consulting framework with no authorial origin, no intellectual credibility signal, and no differentiation from the dozens of AI maturity models already competing in this space.

### Biggest Strengths

- SIMA-Flow's FLAI explanation is the site's clearest conceptual writing. The Frame/Learn/Assess/Improve breakdown is specific and defensible.
- The SIMA-Probe subscription tiers show operational thinking — actual product architecture rather than abstract description.
- The About page's foundational research section (nine source frameworks, what was incorporated, what gaps were addressed) is the most credible content on the site and should be significantly elevated.
- Component naming (Probe, Flow, Kit, Ascend) is distinctive enough to carry an ecosystem identity — the naming system works, the explanation of how they connect does not.

### Biggest Weaknesses

1. The book is invisible. The intellectual foundation is unacknowledged.
2. The home page hero copy ("Implement AI at the organizational level while Controlling Risk and Maximizing Value") is indistinguishable from any AI consulting firm.
3. No ecosystem diagram exists that shows how the five components connect, sequence, and feed each other.
4. The About page positions the framework as a disaster-prevention tool. This is defensible as a secondary message but damaging as the primary framing — it subordinates a maturity architecture to a fear narrative.
5. The site contains two typos in the home page hero alone ("organizaitonal," "Contolling") which damage the credibility that the framework's intellectual rigor is meant to establish.
6. Terminology is inconsistent. "AI Dimensions" on some pages, "Domains" in the book, "AI Dimensions" becomes "Domains" in some copy blocks without explanation.
7. The home page lists only four ecosystem components despite SIMA-Probe being its own nav item and arguably the most operationally important component.
8. The call to action throughout the site is "Schedule a Conversation" — appropriate for a consulting firm, wrong for a framework platform with training programs, downloadable tools, and a self-service assessment.

## 2. Website-to-Book Alignment Review

### Where the Site Aligns

- The five domains (Strategy, Governance, Data, People, Technology) are consistently named across all pages. This is the book's core diagnostic structure and it holds on the site.
- The six capability levels are represented correctly, though the Probe page calls Level 1 "Exploring" while the Core page labels Level 1 "Initial" and Level 2 "Exploring." This is a direct inconsistency that must be resolved — the book's numbering is the source of record.
- FLAI is explained on the Flow page in language that aligns with the book's framing. The "why FLAI vs. PDIA" note is present and correct.
- The Kit's division between macro (Capabilities Toolkit) and micro (Project-Level Toolbox) mirrors the book's Core Cycle / FLAI Cycle structure correctly.

### Where the Site Drifts

**Concept drift — maturity vs. implementation:**
The book's central argument is that activity does not equal capability and capability does not equal maturity. That distinction is the intellectual spine. The site almost never makes this distinction explicitly. It speaks of "AI implementation," "AI transformation," and "accelerating AI adoption" — all activity-oriented language that bypasses the maturity argument entirely.

**Tone drift — systems to consulting:**
The book is analytically oriented. It argues from organizational behavior and failure patterns. The site has drifted toward consulting pitch language: "sustainable value," "maximum impact and ROI," "accelerate your AI transformation journey." These are not maturity-oriented phrases. They are conversion-oriented phrases, and they actively weaken the intellectual positioning.

**Simplification drift — governance:**
The book makes a specific and important distinction between operational governance and performative governance. The site reduces governance to "compliance," "oversight," and "ethical standards" — which is governance theater language, ironically the exact failure mode the book documents.

**Framework dilution — the About page:**
The About page builds its case around Amazon and Tesla failure stories. These are illustrative examples of AI failures, not diagnostic evidence for the maturity gap argument. Leading with famous disasters positions SIMA360 as a risk mitigation product, not a maturity architecture. An executive who doesn't feel at risk of "becoming the next Amazon recruiting disaster" has no reason to engage. The book's argument — that even organizations doing AI reasonably well are operating below reliable maturity thresholds — is far more threatening and far more relevant to the actual buyer.

### Where Concepts Weaken

- "Controlled implementation of AI" (home page eyebrow) reduces a maturity architecture to a project management constraint.
- "A compass and a playbook" (home page body copy) is vague metaphor language that a framework platform should not be using.
- "Join leading organizations using SIMA360™ to drive strategic AI transformation" (home page CTA) — there are no named organizations, no case evidence, and the phrasing is pure AI consulting boilerplate.
- The AI Capability Levels on the Core page describe each level as what organizations are doing at that level, not what capability conditions define the level. This inverts the diagnostic logic: the levels should describe the organizational state, not the activity pattern.

## 3. SIMA Ecosystem Clarity Review

### SIMA-Core

**Current state:** Adequately described as the structural foundation. The three models (Dimensions, Capability Levels, Tool Categories) are explained with appropriate depth.

**Critical problem:** The page title says "Core Elements of SIMA360" but the subhead says "three core models." The framing oscillates between describing Core as the whole framework and describing it as one component of the framework. A user who reads Core before reading any other page will come away confused about whether they have read the framework itself or just its foundation.

**Specific fix needed:** Core must be framed explicitly as the static conceptual architecture — the vocabulary and structure everything else operates within. One clear sentence: "SIMA-Core is the conceptual vocabulary of the framework. It does not prescribe action. It defines the dimensions, levels, and tool categories that every other component uses to operate."

### SIMA-Probe

**Current state:** The best-explained component. The subscription tier architecture shows real operational thinking. The integration description ("Probe supports the Assess Capabilities phase of SIMA-Flow") is the site's only explicit inter-component connection statement.

**Critical problem:** The maturity levels on the Probe page start at Level 1 = Exploring, while the Core page lists Level 1 = Initial and Level 2 = Exploring. This is a direct numerical inconsistency that will confuse every practitioner who reads both pages. The book's level structure is the source of record — Core and Probe must match exactly.

**Secondary problem:** The "Adaptive AI Evaluation Features" section describes AI capabilities (contextual tailoring, document intelligence) without connecting them back to the framework logic. Why does adaptive evaluation matter from a maturity standpoint? The answer — because behavioral maturity cannot be assessed with static yes/no questions — is implicit in the book but absent from the page.

### SIMA-Flow

**Current state:** The strongest conceptual page on the site. FLAI is explained with appropriate specificity. The Core Cycle / FLAI Cycle distinction (macro vs. micro) is structurally sound.

**Critical problem:** Flow is positioned as "the execution engine" — which makes it sound like an activity driver rather than an improvement architecture. The book's framing of FLAI as a disciplined learning cycle, not an execution methodology, is more accurate and more defensible. "Execution engine" drifts toward agile delivery language.

**Secondary problem:** The "Why FLAI vs. Plan-Do-Inspect-Adapt?" section uses the correct logic but the wrong comparison. The book compares FLAI to PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act). The site compares it to PDIA (Plan-Do-Inspect-Adapt), which is a less common reference and will confuse readers who know PDCA. Use PDCA as the primary comparison.

### SIMA-Kit

**Current state:** Adequately described at the component level. The macro/micro structure is clear.

**Critical problem:** Kit is described as "the bridge between strategy and execution" — but this is Flow's role, not Kit's. Kit is the resource layer: the templates, rubrics, and playbooks that the Flow cycles consume. If Kit is the bridge, what is Flow? The role distinction between Kit and Flow is the site's most significant structural confusion at the component level.

**Specific fix needed:** Kit = the resource library, stratified by dimension and capability level. Flow = the cycle architecture that determines when and how those resources are deployed. Kit does not bridge anything. It supplies what Flow requires.

### SIMA-Ascend

**Current state:** The most fully operationalized page on the site. The three certification levels, five courses, and prerequisite structure are all clearly defined.

**Critical problem:** The connection to the book is absent. Ascend is the capability-building component — it is where practitioners go to develop the skills the book argues organizations consistently lack. That connection should be explicit. "These courses operationalize the book's argument that organizational AI capability requires deliberate, structured human development — not just tool deployment."

**Secondary problem:** The page headline is "Professional Development & Certification Framework" — which sounds like an HR product. Ascend's identity within the SIMA ecosystem is organizational capability development, which is a strategic function, not a professional development program. The distinction matters for executive buyers.

### Ecosystem Relationships

The five components are catalogued on the site. They are not connected. No diagram, no prose explanation, and no page on the site explains the operational logic of the sequence:

Core defines the vocabulary → Probe applies it diagnostically → Flow structures the improvement cycle → Kit supplies the cycle's resources → Ascend builds the human capability to run the system.

That sentence should appear on the home page. It currently appears nowhere.

## 4. Structural and Navigation Recommendations

### Navigation

**Current nav order:** Home | Probe | Core | Flow | Kit | Ascend | Download Guide | Class List | About

**Problems:**
- Probe appears before Core despite Core being the conceptual foundation everything else depends on. A user who clicks Probe first has no vocabulary for understanding what they are being assessed against.
- "Download Guide" and "Class List" are utility links that do not belong in primary navigation alongside ecosystem components.
- "About" is buried last but contains the site's most credible institutional content (the research foundations section).

**Recommended nav order:** Home | Framework | [Core | Probe | Flow | Kit | Ascend as sub-items or a dropdown] | The Book | About | Get Started

**Rationale:**
- Grouping the five components under "Framework" signals ecosystem cohesion before users click.
- "The Book" as a nav item is the single highest-leverage addition: it establishes the intellectual foundation and differentiates the site from every other AI maturity framework platform.
- "Get Started" replaces "Download Guide" and "Class List" as a unified entry point that routes users to the appropriate component based on their role and starting condition.

### Page Additions Needed

**1. The Book page**
A dedicated page that positions the book as the intellectual origin of SIMA360. Include: the book's central argument, how the five domains and six levels emerged from that argument, why the book is the recommended starting point before engaging any tool or training, and where to get it. This page is the credibility anchor for the entire site.

**2. Ecosystem Overview page (or section)**
A single page or prominent home page section that explains the five-component system as an integrated whole: what each component does, how they connect, and the logical sequence through the ecosystem. An ecosystem diagram is essential here.

**3. Start Here / Get Started page**
A routing page for different entry points: "I want to understand the framework" → Core, "I want to assess my organization" → Probe, "I want to run a project" → Flow + Kit, "I want to build capability" → Ascend, "I want to start with the book" → The Book page. This eliminates navigation confusion without requiring users to understand the ecosystem before they begin.

### Page Restructuring

**Home page:** The current home page treats SIMA360 as an AI consulting product. It needs to be rebuilt around three ideas in sequence: (1) the organizational problem (maturity gap, not AI disasters), (2) the framework as the solution architecture (five components, their logic), (3) the book as the origin and entry point. Strip all generic AI transformation language.

**About page:** The failure stories (Amazon, Tesla) should be moved out of the lead position and into a supporting section. The research foundations section (nine frameworks, comparative analysis) should lead. The intellectual credibility case is more durable than the fear case.

**Core page:** Add an explicit statement that Core is the vocabulary layer — it does not prescribe action. Add a brief ecosystem map showing where Core fits relative to the other four components.

## 6. Visual and Diagram Recommendations

### The Ecosystem Diagram (Missing — Critical)

The site references the SIMA-Core cycle SVG but no diagram shows the full five-component ecosystem. This is the most important missing visual. The diagram must show:

- The five components as a connected system, not a list
- The logical flow: Core (foundation) → Probe (assessment) → Flow (cycle architecture) → Kit (resources) → Ascend (capability)
- The feedback loop: Ascend-developed practitioners run better Probe assessments and Flow cycles, which generates better Kit usage, which improves Core application
- Entry and re-entry points for different user types

**Design direction:** Systems-oriented, not decorative. Think circuit diagram or architecture blueprint aesthetic, not marketing infographic. Use clean geometry. No glowing nodes, no neural network imagery, no gradient blobs.

### The Maturity Level Progression Diagram (Present, Needs Revision)

The Core page has a capability levels description but no visual progression map. A clean horizontal or vertical progression showing the six levels, their defining organizational characteristics (not their activity descriptions), and the domain-specific maturity indicators at each level would significantly improve comprehension.

### The Book-to-Framework Bridge Visual

A simple two-column or two-circle diagram on the Book page or home page showing:
- Left: The Book — conceptual foundation, diagnostic framework, maturity argument
- Right: SIMA360 Website — operational extension, five components, implementation system

This is the relationship diagram the site needs most after the ecosystem diagram.

### Visual Identity Direction

The site is currently built on a generic v0.app scaffold. For a framework platform that claims intellectual rigor, the visual identity should signal:
- Structured thinking (grid-based layouts, consistent spacing, clear hierarchy)
- Operational precision (monospace or serif type elements for framework terms, clear component labeling)
- Institutional seriousness (restrained color palette, no gradient hero sections, no animation that doesn't carry information)

Avoid: glowing AI brain graphics, particle network backgrounds, "transformation" stock photography, blue-gradient hero sections.

## 8. Final Strategic Assessment

**Does the website currently operationalize the book effectively?**
No. The book does not appear on the site. The book's central argument (the maturity gap, the distinction between activity and capability) is not present in any substantive form. The site explains five framework components. It does not make the argument that necessitates the framework.

**Does the ecosystem feel coherent?**
Partially. Each component is described adequately in isolation. The connections between components are either absent or incorrect (Kit/Flow role confusion). No user journey connects the components logically. No diagram makes the system visible as a system.

**Does the website increase confidence in the framework?**
Not as much as it should. The research foundation on the About page is genuinely strong. The FLAI explanation is sound. But the home page hero and the About page lead both undermine the intellectual positioning — the first with consulting boilerplate, the second with disaster-prevention fear narrative. An executive evaluating frameworks needs to trust the analytical rigor before they will commit. That trust is not currently being built.

**Does the site feel differentiated?**
Marginally. The five-component naming system is distinctive. FLAI is a genuinely different framing than PDCA/Scrum. But the messaging does not surface these differentiators clearly. "Comprehensive framework for implementing AI" is not differentiated. "The only AI maturity framework that provides a structured improvement path, not just a maturity description" would be.

**Does the site feel operationally serious?**
Less serious than the book. The book's voice is analytical, specific, and behaviorally-oriented. The site's voice oscillates between consulting pitch and product documentation. The gap is audible.

**Does the site sound like a maturity ecosystem or generic AI consulting?**
Currently: generic AI consulting with a framework catalog attached. Target: a maturity ecosystem with an intellectual origin, a logical architecture, and clear operational pathways.

**The one-sentence verdict:**
The site describes the framework components but does not yet embody the framework's intellectual character — and without the book as its visible foundation, it has no credible origin story to differentiate it from the AI consulting landscape it is trying to transcend.
